# Leadership Interview Questions

## Team Management

### Q: How do you align multiple distributed teams toward shared goals?
**A:** Quarterly roadmaps tied to company OKRs, with each team owning specific outcomes. Weekly cross-team syncs surface dependencies early. RFCs for decisions affecting multiple teams require stakeholder review. Build relationships so leads communicate directly, not just through me.

### Q: How do you handle underperformance?
**A:** First diagnose: skills gap, motivation, personal issues, or role mismatch? Direct conversation about observations and impact. Clear improvement plan with metrics and timeline. Increase 1:1s, pair with strong teammates, remove blockers. If no improvement after genuine effort, honest conversation about fit.

### Q: How do you prioritize across teams?
**A:** Impact × urgency matrix aligned with company goals. Regular syncs with Product to understand customer needs. Protect 20% for tech debt and reliability. Make prioritization transparent so teams understand context. Willing to make hard calls and own the consequences.

## Technical Leadership

### Q: How do you balance innovation with reliability?
**A:** 70% product work, 20% reliability/tech debt, 10% innovation. Track on-call burden - if too high, invest in reliability first. New innovations (like OrioleDB) start as opt-in beta, gather data, then expand. Feature flags enable shipping fast with safe rollback.

### Q: How do you make architectural decisions?
**A:** Written RFCs with problem statement, options considered, and recommendation. Required review from affected teams. Time-box decisions - don't let perfect be enemy of good. Document decisions for future context. Revisit when circumstances change.

### Q: How do you stay technical while managing multiple teams?
**A:** Participate in design reviews and code reviews for major features. Keep one area where I go deep. Regular 1:1s with tech leads focused on technical challenges. Read the on-call reports and postmortems. Build systems so I get context without micromanaging.

## Your Approach

### Q: Describe your management philosophy
**A:** Context over control. Hire smart people, give them context (strategy, customer needs, constraints), let them figure out solutions. Radical transparency about what's working and not. Protect team focus. Take responsibility for failures, give credit for successes.

### Q: How do you build team culture remotely?
**A:** Async-first communication for work, sync for relationships. Regular video 1:1s focused on the person, not just work. Virtual social events that respect time zones. Quarterly in-person gatherings if budget allows. Clear written norms so everyone knows expectations.

### Q: How do you handle disagreements with leadership?
**A:** Disagree and commit, but only after clearly articulating my position and listening to theirs. Document my concerns for the record. Once decision is made, execute fully - don't undermine. Revisit if data proves the decision wrong, without saying "I told you so."

## Supabase-Specific

### Q: How would you approach leading teams across OrioleDB, Multigres, and Wrappers?
**A:** Each has different maturity and technical domains. Spend first 30 days understanding each team's challenges, roadmap, and people. Find common infrastructure needs across teams. Establish consistent practices (project tracking, on-call, code review) while respecting team autonomy. Build relationships with product counterparts.

### Q: What would be your 90-day plan?
**A:** 
- **Days 1-30:** Listen and learn. 1:1s with everyone. Understand current roadmaps, challenges, and team dynamics. Build relationships with Product and other Engineering leaders.
- **Days 31-60:** Identify gaps in process, staffing, or technical direction. Propose improvements. Start hiring for critical gaps. Begin contributing to technical decisions.
- **Days 61-90:** Execute on improvements. Establish regular operating rhythm. Demonstrate value through delivered results and improved team effectiveness.

### Q: Why should we hire someone learning Postgres for Head of Databases?
**A:** You're hiring for leadership, not to write database code. I bring: proven team leadership, developer empathy from building products, fast learning velocity, and fresh perspective. The technical depth I'm acquiring rapidly; the judgment and leadership approach took years. I'll surround myself with Postgres experts and learn from them while providing organizational leadership.
